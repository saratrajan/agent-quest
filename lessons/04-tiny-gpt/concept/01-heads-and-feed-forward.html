One attention head learns one way of looking back. Language needs several at once: which word a pronoun refers to, which service a symptom belongs to, where the current line began. A transformer runs several heads side by side on the same input and joins their outputs.

The sizes are arranged so the result is as wide as the input. With vectors of 128 numbers and 4 heads, each head produces 128 ÷ 4 = 32 numbers per position; concatenated they make 128 again, and a final linear layer mixes them.

Attention only gathers: its output is a blend of earlier positions' values. Something has to work on what was gathered. That is the feed-forward layer: a small two-layer network applied to each position on its own, expanding to four times the width and back.

One of each makes a block. A GPT is a stack of identical blocks: gather, process, gather, process. Later blocks attend over the already-processed outputs of earlier ones, which is how the model builds up from characters to words to the shape of a whole incident note.

Two more ingredients complete the model:

  • Position embeddings. Session 3 showed that attention has no sense of order. So the model learns a vector for each position, 0 to 127, and adds it to the token's vector at the very start. After that, "P" at position 0 and "P" at position 40 are different inputs.
  • The output layer. After the last block, a linear layer turns each position's 128 numbers back into one score per token in the vocabulary, the logits from session 2.
