Scores can be any numbers. Two steps turn them into a single measure of how wrong the model was.

Step 1, softmax: scores to probabilities. Raise e to each score, then divide by the total so they sum to 1.

pi = ezi / Σj ezj

Step 2, cross-entropy: the loss. Look only at the probability given to the token that actually came next, and take minus its logarithm.

L = − log ptarget

Work it once by hand. After "payments are", three candidate next tokens have scores 2.0, 1.0 and 0.1:

If the real next token was "failing", the model put 66% on it and the loss is 0.417. If it was "resolved", the model put only 10% there and pays 2.317. Being confidently wrong is expensive; that is the property that makes this loss useful.

Move the scores and the target below and watch both numbers.

Two reference points worth memorising:

  • A perfect prediction has probability 1 and loss 0.
  • A uniform guess over V tokens has loss log V. For our 77 characters that is 4.344. A model scoring above that is worse than knowing nothing.

The loss you see during training is the average of this number over every prediction in the batch.

Source for the neural approach to next-word prediction: Bengio, Ducharme, Vincent and Jauvin (2003), A Neural Probabilistic Language Model.
